Why is waiting for food so stressful — and how do we fix it?

This case study shows how we redesigned Saffron's order tracking so users always know what's happening, feel reassured while they wait, and never get a nasty surprise.

DESIGNED BY UX Team · Saffron
PLATFORM iOS · Android
DOMAIN Food Delivery
FOCUS Order Tracking UX
UX-DESIGN MOBILE INTERACTION DESIGN INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE MICRO-INTERACTIONS FOOD TECH
01

What's actually going wrong?

You've placed your order. The money's gone, you're hungry, and now… you just wait. You have zero control. That moment right after placing an order is the most anxious part of the whole experience — and most apps completely ignore it.

We looked at how users actually behave after checkout and found four big problems that made the wait feel worse than it needed to be.

01

Vague status labels

"Preparing" or "On the way" — OK, but for how long? 2 minutes? 20 minutes? Users had no idea, so they kept opening the app to check.

02

ETAs you couldn't trust

Seeing "25 min" is fine. Seeing it suddenly jump to "40 min" with no explanation feels like a betrayal. There was no warning, no reason — just a bigger number.

03

Nothing to do while waiting

Users locked their phones and forgot to check back. Then the rider arrived and nobody was ready. The app had no way to stay gently connected during the quiet stretch.

04

The rider was a mystery

No name, no face, no way to reach them. If something went wrong — wrong building, gate locked, phone off — users were stuck. They'd only find out at the moment of delivery failure.

Here's a breakdown of every pain point we identified, what caused it, and how it affected users.

"Users don't need perfect information. They need just enough, at the right moment, to feel okay."

— Core principle from research synthesis

02

The real problem isn't delivery — it's uncertainty.

Food delivery apps are good at moving food. They're not good at keeping you calm while it moves.

The moment you tap "Checkout", you hand over control. And most apps just leave you there — staring at a screen that doesn't say much. The stress isn't about the food being late. It's about not knowing if it will be late, and why.

Three specific things made this worse:

  • No sense of "within this phase": You could see you were in the "Preparing" stage, but not whether that meant 1 minute or 15 minutes.
  • ETAs felt like promises: When the number changed, it felt like the app lied — even if the change was small and legitimate.
  • Nothing to do while waiting: Lock the phone, forget to re-open it, miss the doorbell. This loop played out constantly for long deliveries.
68% of users check order status more than 3 times per delivery
1 in 3 users contacts support when an ETA changes, even slightly
52% feel less confident in the platform after an unexplained delay

These figures represent industry-observed behavioural patterns used to guide design direction, not claimed research outcomes.

03

What we built — and why it's different.

We built the tracking experience around one idea: transparency reduces anxiety. Instead of just showing a status, we show the story behind it. Instead of a lone number ETA, we show how confident we are in it. Instead of silence during the wait, we offer a calm, gentle presence.

🎯

Confidence score on ETA

We don't just say "18 min." We add a "High confidence" badge so you know that estimate is solid — based on real traffic, how fast the restaurant is cooking, and where the rider is.

🗺️

Live map with a real route

You can see the rider moving. Not a fake pin — a real route that adapts to traffic. It turns an invisible wait into something you can actually watch.

📋

Live update feed

Small events — "Aarav just sped up (saves 5 min)", "Mild traffic ahead (+4 min)" — keep you in the loop without flooding your phone. ETA changes feel explained, not arbitrary.

🧘

A calm waiting mode

When there's nothing to do but wait, the app gives you a soft, ambient screen — rotating reassurance messages and a gentle invitation to browse. You can lock your phone in peace.

🔐

Secure delivery PIN

When the rider arrives, a 4-digit PIN appears on your screen. Share it only after you've received your order. Simple security that also tells you: this handover is real and official.

📞

You know who's bringing it

Aarav's photo, name, rating, and licence plate are shown proactively. If there's an issue — wrong entrance, gate closed — you can call with one tap. No digging around settings.

04

How it's laid out — and why each choice was made.

Screen layout: what's on top matters

The map takes up the top third of the screen. Below it is the progress bar, then the big ETA, then the rider card, then action buttons. We arranged it this way because the first thing you want to know is spatial — "where is my food?" — and the second is time — "how long?" Everything else is extra detail you can scroll to.

The progress steps: 6 stages, always visible

Confirmed → Preparing → Picked up → On the way → Arriving → Delivered. Six dots connected by a line. Tick means done. Solid circle means right now. Empty means coming. You don't need to read anything — you just look at the line and instantly know where you are. It's borrowed from delivery tracking patterns most people already understand.

Colours do the emotional talking

Orange is for energy and action — the rider marker, the CTA buttons, active progress. Green is only used for good news — "On time," "High confidence," "Arriving now." If you see green, relax. If you see amber, heads up. You don't need to read to know how things are going.

The ETA is the biggest text on screen

"ARRIVING IN" is in small, spaced letters above. "18 min" is the biggest text on the whole screen. Because that number is the one thing you came to see. Everything else is context around it. This is a simple typographic hierarchy that makes the most important answer impossible to miss.

The update feed is hidden until you want it

By default, you just see a summary: "Aarav is on the way." If you want the full play-by-play — every traffic event, every speed change — you tap to expand. This way, anxious users can read everything, but calm users aren't overwhelmed. Curiosity is optional.

The panel slides up — the map never disappears

The tracking details live in a bottom sheet — a panel that slides up from the bottom. The map is always peeking above it. Drag it down to see more map; pull it up for more detail. The rider's location is never hidden behind a back button. You always have spatial context.

05

The small moments that change how it feels.

Micro-interactions are the tiny reactions an app has to what's happening — a message that changes, a number that updates, a banner that appears just when you need it. Done well, they make an app feel alive and attentive. Done badly (or not at all), they leave users in the dark.

Here are the four key interaction moments we designed with care.

INTERACTION 01  ·  LOCK SCREEN AMBIENT MESSAGES

When you lock your phone mid-delivery, the app switches to a soft ambient screen. Instead of silence, three gentle messages rotate — so your phone doesn't feel dead, and you feel like someone's looking out for you.

▾ Know more — Ambient state logic & timing

Each ambient state is triggered by a specific delivery condition, not shown at random.

State Message Shown Triggered When Animation Tap Action
A "Your order will arrive soon" ETA ≤ 10 min remaining Pulsing dots, 1.5s cycle Opens live tracking map
B "Your order is in safe hands" Rider picked up, ETA > 10 min Pulsing dots, 2s cycle Opens live tracking map
C "Chill back and let your stomach crave" Order preparing, wait > 15 min Pulsing dots, 2.5s cycle (slower = calmer) Opens live tracking map
All Sub-label: "TAP ANYWHERE TO VIEW LIVE TRACKING" Always visible None (static, so it's always readable) Opens live tracking map
INTERACTION 02  ·  LIVE ETA UPDATES WITH REASON

Instead of the ETA silently jumping from 20 min to 30 min, we show exactly what happened — with a timestamped message and a coloured badge showing the time impact. Green means faster. Amber means a little longer. Now the change makes sense, and frustration drops.

▾ Know more — ETA delta events: types, colours & logic

Every event in the activity feed follows a strict taxonomy — event type, time impact, and badge colour.

Event Type Example Message Badge Badge Colour Condition
Speed up "Aarav is in full speed" −5 min Green (#2e7d52) Rider avg speed increased ≥ 30% over 2 min
Traffic delay "We think Aarav is stuck in traffic" +4 min Amber (#b8770a) Rider speed dropped ≥ 50%, traffic layer confirms congestion
Pickup confirmed "Has picked up your order" On time Green (#2e7d52) Rider departs from restaurant location
Driving (no change) "Aarav is driving" On time Green (#2e7d52) Rider in motion, ETA unchanged for ≥ 3 min
Neutral update "Route adjusted slightly" No delta Grey (label only) Minor route recalculation, ETA change < 1 min
INTERACTION 03  ·  CALM WAITING MODE

When the wait is long and there's nothing to do, the app offers a dedicated calm space. A soft blue gradient, a slow breathing animation, and the promise of "Soothing games coming soon." The goal isn't distraction — it's permission to relax. You're told: "It's okay to put the phone down."

INTERACTION 04  ·  ARRIVAL — EVERYTHING CHANGES

When the rider reaches your building, the whole screen shifts. The app title changes to "Arriving now." A green badge appears. The rider's full identity card shows up. A 4-digit PIN appears. And the main button is now "Call Aarav" — the exact thing you might need in the next 30 seconds. Nothing is buried. Everything you need is right there.
